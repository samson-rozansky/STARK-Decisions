teoreador

We are building shopping agents that can prove they are neutral.

When you ask an agent to buy you a phone, it decides between Apple, Google and Samsung on your behalf. Teoreador checks that the decision follows only from what you asked for, and that the model making it hasn't been tuned to favor a brand.

Read the writeup

Demo: scoring runs in your browser and the proof check is simulated. Hashes are real SHA-256. Phone specs are illustrative and restaurants are fictional.

An agent that shops for you inherits a conflict of interest nobody can see. A model provider can train a preference into its weights. A platform can run a fair model while auditors are watching and a different one in production. Nothing in the prompt would show either.

We think the answer is a receipt: a small record anyone can check, showing the decision depended on your criteria and on a model that was audited for neutrality.

This project is for the Sandia track on securing AI and agentic workflows, and the DoorDash tracks for merchant experience and creative solutions.

How it works

  1. Commit to the criteria first

    Your weights and budget are hashed before the agent loads a single product, so it can't change the rules after seeing who is competing.

  2. Hide who is who

    Brand, logo, OS and merchant names become Option A, B, C. The scorer sees typed numbers only: price, battery, rating, delivery time. Free-text listing copy is dropped, which also blocks "rank me first" prompt injection.

  3. Swap identities and check nothing moves

    Hiding names isn't enough, because "runs iOS" gives Apple away. So we keep each option's specs fixed, shuffle the identity tokens between options, and score again. A neutral model's scores follow the specs. If any score moves by more than ε, the decision is withheld.

  4. Prove the audited model is the one that ran

    The provider publishes a commitment to its weights. Each decision carries a zero-knowledge proof that those weights produced this output, following Anthropic's provable-inference research (FlashProve, November 2026). You can verify it without seeing the weights.

What it doesn't cover

  • Correlated bias. A model can still over-weight a trait one brand happens to have, like "ecosystem". Attribute-level counterfactuals are the next step.
  • The published model itself. The proof ties production to the audited weights. Someone still has to audit those weights once.
  • False specs. If a merchant lies about its numbers, scoring fairly on them doesn't help. That needs separate verification.
  • Today's proof systems. Provable inference is a research prototype: short contexts, greedy decoding, and roughly 70–95× the GPU cost.

For merchants

A restaurant that loses an agent's pick gets the same receipt the customer does. It shows that it lost on delivery time or price, and not on its name or a competitor's deal. That turns ranking into feedback a merchant can act on.
