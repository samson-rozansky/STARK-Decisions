STARK-Decisions
SlidesWriteupCode

A shopping agent that researches for you, and can prove it played fair.

An audited decision model narrows the field to three on your criteria alone. Then an open-weights research model investigates each one in a loop. Every step is hashed, chained and signed, so you can check the work instead of trusting it.

Phone specs are illustrative and restaurants are fictional. The phone run is a recorded run of qwen3.5:4b. The dinner run's research text was written for the demo, and its receipt says so. The cheating server is simulated in your browser.

An agent that shops for you inherits a conflict of interest nobody can see. A model provider can train a brand preference into its weights. A platform can run a fair model while auditors watch and a different one in production. People already sense this: it's a big part of why AI that "thinks for you" feels untrustworthy.

Our answer is to keep the person in charge and make the agent's work checkable. You set the criteria. The agent shortlists and researches. You decide, holding a receipt that proves how the shortlist was made.

How it works

  1. Commit to your criteria

    Your weights are hashed before any product is scored, so the rules can't change after the agent sees who's competing.

  2. Decision model picks three

    A small, open, deterministic scorer (decision.js) ranks options on specs alone. An identity-swap test then shuffles brand, OS and name between options and re-scores. If any score moves, brand influenced the result.

  3. Research loop on the top three

    Qwen3.5 asks the next most useful question about each pick, answers it, and repeats. It runs at temperature 0 with pinned weights, so every step is reproducible. Each step's prompt and output are hashed into a chain.

  4. Signed receipt

    The policy, catalog, scorer code, model weights digest and research chain are hashed and signed with Ed25519. Your browser re-runs the decision model and checks everything. node verify.mjs --rerun re-runs the research loop too.

Run it yourself

ollama pull qwen3.5:27b          # or qwen3.5:4b on a 16 GB laptop
MODEL=qwen3.5:27b node server.mjs
open http://localhost:8787
node verify.mjs receipts/latest.json --rerun

Qwen3.5-27B needs roughly 20 GB of memory. On our 16 GB laptops we run qwen3.5:4b through the identical code path; point OLLAMA_URL at a bigger machine for 27B.

What it doesn't cover yet

  • Proof without re-running. Today, verifying the research step means re-running it on the same weights. Zero-knowledge proof of inference (as in Anthropic's FlashProve research) would let anyone verify without the weights or a GPU.
  • Correlated bias. A scorer can still over-weight a trait one brand happens to have. Attribute-level counterfactuals are next.
  • False specs. Fair scoring on false numbers is still wrong. Catalog data needs its own verification.
