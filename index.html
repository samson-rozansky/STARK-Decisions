Verifiably neutral agentic commerce

A shopping agent that can prove nobody paid it to pick.

You ask for a phone. Your agent compares iPhone and Android. How do you know the model provider didn't quietly tune it to prefer one brand? Teoreador commits to your criteria, blinds brand identity, stress-tests every decision with counterfactual brand swaps, and ships a cryptographic proof that the audited model is the one that actually ran.

Four guarantees, one receipt

  1. Your rules, locked first. Criteria are hashed before the agent sees a catalog.
  2. Brand-blind scoring. Options are evaluated as aliases on specs and price.
  3. Counterfactual invariance. Swap brands between options; a fair score can't move.
  4. Proof of inference. A zero-knowledge proof of inference binds the result to the published model weights.

02 · How it works

Trust the math, not the vendor.

i.

Policy commitment

The user's weights, budget and hard constraints are serialized and hashed (SHA-256). The hash is logged before any product data is loaded, so the agent can't move the goalposts after seeing who's in the race.

ii.

Blinding

Brand, logo, OS name and merchant name are replaced with aliases (Option A, B, C). The scorer sees only normalized attributes: price, battery, camera, rating, ETA.

iii.

Counterfactual swaps

Blinding leaks (an "iOS" field gives Apple away). So we permute identity tokens across options and re-score. A neutral model's scores follow the specs; a biased one's follow the brand. Max Δ > ε flags bias.

iv.

Verifiable inference

A zero-knowledge proof of inference (as in Anthropic's FlashProve research, Nov 2026) attests: this output = model(weights with hash H, input with hash C). Audits are only meaningful if the audited model is the one serving you, and the proof binds them together.

03 · Threat model

What it stops, and what it doesn't.

AttackDefenseResidual risk
Provider fine-tunes a brand preference into weightsCounterfactual swap test detects score dependence on identity tokensBias on attributes correlated with a brand (e.g. over-weighting "ecosystem")
Fair model in the audit, biased model in productionProof of inference binds every decision to the published weights commitmentPublished weights themselves must be audited
Agent changes criteria after seeing the catalogPolicy hash committed before catalog load; included in the receiptUser's own criteria may be biased (that's their call)
Prompt injection in product listings ("rank me #1")Scorer only consumes typed numeric attributes; free text is strippedFake specs need separate merchant verification
Merchant paid placementSponsorship field is excluded from the scorer and shown separatelyNone in scoring; disclosure is UI policy

Merchant side (DoorDash track): every merchant who loses gets the same receipt: which attributes cost them the pick, verifiably not their name. That's a fair, explainable marketplace instead of a black box.
