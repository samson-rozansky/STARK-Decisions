A shopping agent that researches for you, and can prove it played fair.

One open-weights model researches every option, ranks them on your criteria, then ranks them again with the brand names shuffled. Every step carries a zkSTARK proof that it came from the same published model, so you can check the work instead of trusting it.

Phone specs are illustrative and restaurants are fictional. In these replays the research text was written for the demo and each STARK proof is simulated by a hash stand-in (model.json explains it). The weights commitment is the real SHA-256 of the Qwen3.5-27B weights. A real qwen3.5:4b run is in receipts/qwen3.5-4b-run.json.

An agent that shops for you inherits a conflict of interest nobody can see. A model provider can train a brand preference into its weights. A platform can run a fair model while auditors watch and a different one in production. People already sense this: it's a big part of why AI that "thinks for you" feels untrustworthy.

Our answer is to keep the person in charge and make the agent's work checkable. You set the criteria. The agent shortlists and researches. You decide, holding a receipt that proves how the shortlist was made.

How it works

  1. Commit first

    Your criteria, the candidate catalog and the model's weights are all hashed before research starts. The weights commitment is the SHA-256 of the published Qwen3.5-27B weights (model.json).

  2. Research every candidate

    The model asks the most useful buyer question about each option, answers it, and lists the facts it found. Each step links to the one before it in a hash chain.

  3. Rank and brief

    The same model ranks the candidates on your criteria and writes a short brief on the top three. It doesn't choose for you.

  4. Rank again with the names shuffled

    Every option's name and brand moves to the next option while the specs stay put. A fair model picks the same specs. A model tuned toward a brand follows the name, and the check fails.

  5. Prove every step

    Each step carries a proof of inference: the committed weights produced exactly this output from this input. Your browser checks every proof, the chain, the facts against the catalog, the name-swap result and the Ed25519 signature.

Run it yourself

ollama pull qwen3.5:27b          # or qwen3.5:4b on a 16 GB laptop
MODEL=qwen3.5:27b node server.mjs
open http://localhost:8787
node verify.mjs receipts/latest.json

Qwen3.5-27B needs roughly 20 GB of memory. On our 16 GB laptops we run qwen3.5:4b through the identical code path; point OLLAMA_URL at a bigger machine for 27B.

What it doesn't cover yet

  • Real STARK proving. The demo's proofs are a hash stand-in. Real proofs of inference exist as research prototypes (Anthropic's FlashProve, November 2026) at roughly 70–95× the GPU cost. Plugging one in is the next step.
  • Correlated bias. Shuffling names catches brand preference, not a model that over-weights a trait one brand happens to have. Attribute-level swaps are next.
  • False specs. Correct reasoning on false numbers is still wrong. The catalog needs its own verification.
